# The short version

<p class="lede">If you only remember five things. Pick your age.</p>

A short list of confident advice is exactly what this site is usually suspicious of — a claim with no working shown. So every point below says how sure we are and links back to its evidence, weak spots included. **If a point here and its page ever seem to disagree, trust the page.**

## For ages 8–11

1. **Never start smoking or vaping.** Almost everyone who smokes for life started young. Not starting is the one big health choice where doing nothing is the winning move. *How sure: near-certain.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/smoking/)
2. **Move, in any way you enjoy.** The biggest boost comes from going from barely moving to moving a bit — and it makes you feel better today, not just when you are old. *How sure: high.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/movement/)
3. **Protect your sleep.** It is the cheapest way to make tomorrow go better — a steadier mood and an easier time concentrating. *How sure: strong for how you feel tomorrow; weaker for anything long-term.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/sleep/)
4. **Slip, slop, slap.** A shirt, sunscreen and a hat — and shade when the sun is strong. It was tested in a proper experiment, and it worked. *How sure: high.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/sun/)
5. **Remember who wins at gambling games.** Games of chance — including paying for "mystery boxes" in video games — are built so the maker comes out ahead. You are not unlucky; it is maths. *How sure: near-certain.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/gambling/)

## For ages 11–16

1. **Don't start nicotine — cigarettes or vapes.** Your teens are exactly when the addiction is designed to catch you, and not starting removes almost all the future risk at no cost. (Vapes are far less harmful than cigarettes — but for someone who has never smoked, starting is all loss.) *How sure: near-certain.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/smoking/)
2. **No amount of alcohol is good for you — and for you, the danger is tonight.** At your age the real risks are same-night: injuries, accidents, things that can't be undone. *How sure: high.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/alcohol/)
3. **Gambling apps are built so you lose.** The house edge — the share of every bet the game keeps — is chosen in advance and published. You are not unlucky; you are on the wrong side of the maths. *How sure: near-certain.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/gambling/)
4. **Move in any way you'll keep doing.** Leaving the least-active group is the biggest gain there is, and it lifts your mood within weeks. *How sure: high.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/movement/)
5. **Protect your sleep with your surroundings, not your willpower.** Regular hours, a dark room, dim screens in the hour before bed, and no energy drinks in the afternoon. Your late body clock is biology, not laziness. *How sure: strong for mood and focus; weaker for anything long-term.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/sleep/)

## For ages 16+

1. **Don't smoke — and if you do, stop, the earlier the better.** Stopping before 40 avoids the large majority of the extra risk, and stopping before 30 almost all of it. Medicines plus support make quitting far more likely. *How sure: near-certain.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/smoking/)
2. **Move more, starting from the bottom.** The biggest benefit of all is leaving the least-active group, and most of it arrives at far less activity than the round-number targets suggest. *How sure: high.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/movement/)
3. **Don't let excess weight arrive.** Excess body fat causes serious disease, and the body defends it once gained — so change the defaults: what is in the cupboard, and how big the portion is. *How sure: moderate — strong on the harm, weak on which diet.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/food/)
4. **With alcohol, less is better — all the way down to none.** There is no amount that is good for you; the risk starts at the first drink and rises with every one, though a single drink adds only a small risk. *How sure: high.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/alcohol/)
5. **Use your voice and your vote.** The biggest health gains have come from changing shared rules and surroundings — smoke-free laws, minimum alcohol prices, the sugar levy. Which measures are worth their trade-offs is yours to decide. *How sure: high.* [Why we think so →](/slogans/collective/)

## Why these five?

Each list picks the points most likely to matter at that age — the ten-year clock weighs more heavily for younger readers, the seventy-year clock for older ones.
